The Transition

"The work that wouldn't leave my head wasn't the code. It was the question behind the code."

I started out as a software engineer. At Ernst & Young, I was brought in to build and test enterprise systems — and I was good at it. But I kept noticing things that weren't in my brief.

A workflow that everyone followed without questioning why it existed. Patterns in the chatbot data that suggested users weren't behaving the way the team assumed. A broken process that, when you looked closely, wasn't a code problem at all — it was a people-and-incentives problem wearing a technical costume.

I didn't stop at noticing. I went deeper, then flagged what I found. And at some point, the question behind the code started to matter more to me than the code itself.

That realization led me to Arizona State University's W. P. Carey School of Business. I wanted to formalise what I was already doing instinctively — translating messy, ambiguous situations into clear decisions. I graduated with a 4.0 GPA and a capstone project that won the Outstanding Capstone Team Award — not just for what we built, but for how clearly we communicated the story in the data.

I'm now looking for a team that values that combination: the technical fluency to understand what the data is actually saying, and the strategic instinct to know what to do with it.
